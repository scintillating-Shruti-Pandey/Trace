Part 2 · The Problem We Are Solving

💥 Why can't the teacher just say "Why didn't you use standard ChatGPT or LangChain?"

This is the #1 question teachers ask. Here is the exact reason why standard text AI fails in physical engineering:

❌ What Standard Text RAG Does

• Takes a PDF and cuts it into dumb chunks of 500 words.
• Slices directly through tables! The bolt name is in chunk #1, and the torque number is lost in chunk #2.
• Cannot see video! It has zero idea what the mechanic's hands are doing.
• When asked about a video, text RAG gets 0.0% accuracy.

✅ What Trace Does Instead

• Structure-Aware Chunking: Keeps tables and headers intact with breadcrumbs (§3.0 > §3.2).
• Multi-Modal: Uses vision AI (SigLIP 2) to look at video frames + text AI (BGE-M3) to understand spoken words.
• Evidence Graph: 154 pre-audited bridges connecting video timestamps to manual sections.
• Gets 73.3% accuracy (+46.7% boost!).

💡 The "Vocabulary Gap" Analogy (Crucial Concept!)

In videos, mechanics talk casually: "Alright, let's screw down these outer bolts."
In textbooks, engineers write formally: "M10 Grade 8.8 Hex Casing Fastener, 24.5 Nm preload."
Notice that not a single word matches! If you only do keyword search, you find nothing. Trace uses deep dense embeddings (BGE-M3) and human evidence links to understand that these two sentences mean the exact same physical action.

Part 4 · Deep Dive into Models

🧠 The 4 Magic Components & Their Everyday Analogies

1. BGE-M3 (The Brainy Librarian)

What it is: 1024-dimensional dense semantic embedding model.

Simple Analogy: It turns any sentence into a GPS location in "meaning space". If sentence A is "tighten bolt" and sentence B is "torque fastener", they get almost the exact same latitude and longitude!

2. BM25 (The Exact-Match Detective)

What it is: Classic frequency-based lexical keyword matching algorithm.

Simple Analogy: Like `Ctrl + F` on steroids. Dense AI sometimes gets confused between "M8" and "M10". BM25 guarantees that exact part numbers and numbers are never mixed up!

3. SigLIP 2 (The Camera Eyes)

What it is: Google's state-of-the-art vision-language model.

Simple Analogy: It looks at a picture from a video frame and translates what it sees into the exact same coordinates as text. It knows what a "torque wrench" looks like even if nobody speaks in the audio!

4. The Evidence Graph (The Bridges)

What it is: 154 human-audited edges linking video timecodes to manual sections.

Simple Analogy: Imagine sticking a sticky note on a video timestamp saying: "To know why he did this, read page 42, section 3.2 of the pump book!"

Q1 "What exactly is RAG, and why is standard RAG not enough for your project?"
▼
Say this to the teacher:
"Sir/Ma'am, RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Instead of relying solely on an LLM's frozen internal weights, RAG retrieves external evidence from a knowledge base and feeds it into the prompt so the AI answers with verified facts.

However, standard RAG is strictly text-based and ungrounded. In manufacturing plants, procedures are inherently physical and visual. A technician cannot use a system that only retrieves generic text paragraphs; they need sub-second temporal video grounding to see how the action is performed, coupled with the exact manual specification. Naive text RAG fails to bridge video transcripts with technical manuals, resulting in 0% joint retrieval recall."
Q2 "What embedding model did you use and what does 1024-dimensional mean?"
▼
Say this to the teacher:
"We utilized BGE-M3 (BAAI/bge-m3). It is a multi-functional embedding model that outputs a 1024-dimensional dense vector.

When we say 1024-dimensional, it means each procedural video segment and technical manual chunk is mathematically converted into an array of 1024 floating-point numbers. These numbers represent high-level semantic coordinates in a latent conceptual space. We then compute cosine distance (`<=>` operator in PostgreSQL pgvector) between the query vector and document vectors to measure semantic similarity."
Q3 "If you have dense vectors, why do you still need BM25?"
▼
Say this to the teacher:
"Dense embeddings are excellent at semantic generalization, but they suffer from lexical blur on precise alphanumeric engineering codes. For example, a dense model often places 'M8' and 'M10' or '14.5 Nm' and '24.5 Nm' in very close vector proximity because they are grammatically and contextually similar.

In heavy industrial maintenance, confusing M8 with M10 is disastrous. Therefore, we use BM25 as a sparse lexical handle to guarantee that exact fastener dimensions, part numbers, and torque quantities receive an exact keyword match boost."
Q4 "How do you combine BM25 and BGE-M3? What is RRF?"
▼
Say this to the teacher:
"We combine them using Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) with a smoothing constant k=60.

Instead of trying to normalize and add raw score values (which fail because BM25 scores are unbounded positive numbers while cosine scores are bounded between 0 and 1), RRF strictly uses the rank position of the document in each retrieval list using the formula: Score = Sum( 1 / (60 + Rank_i) ).
This produces a robust, parameter-free ranking that gives high priority to items that perform well across both lexical and semantic lanes."
Q5 "What are the 154 evidence links? Why did you need them?"
▼
Say this to the teacher:
"The 154 evidence links represent a pre-audited cross-modal relational graph connecting procedural video intervals directly to authoritative manual chunks.

In our ablation study, we proved that retrieval alone only scored 0.0% joint recall because colloquial spoken language in videos (`'tighten down outer bolts'`) diverges completely from formal engineering text (`'M10 casing bolt 24.5 Nm'`). By traversing this 1-hop evidence graph during search (Link Expansion), our system achieved an immediate +46.7% retrieval lift, raising Joint Recall@10 from 0% to 73.3%."
Q6 "How does your system guarantee zero hallucinations? What is the Honesty Gate?"
▼
Say this to the teacher:
"We enforce two strict architectural mechanisms:

1. Strict Footnote Citations: The synthesis engine is conditioned exclusively on retrieved chunks. Every claim must carry a bracketed footnote citation (`[E1]`, `[E2]`) mapped directly to a verified video timestamp and manual chunk.
2. The Honesty Gate: When a query falls outside the manufacturing domain (such as our test query 'curing time underwater epoxy'), an out-of-domain classifier rejects the query and abstains with 100% precision rather than fabricating an answer."

Q9 "What is IoU in your temporal window refinement?"
▼
Say this to the teacher:
"IoU stands for Intersection over Union. In temporal video grounding, it measures the temporal overlap between our predicted procedure interval $[t_{start}, t_{end}]$ and the human ground-truth span.

IoU = Length(Intersection) / Length(Union)
We enforce an IoU > 0.5 threshold, and automatically offset the playhead by t_start - 2.0s as a lead-in buffer so the operator observes the machine setup state prior to fastener engagement."

Part 7 · Technical Formula & Metric Cheat Sheet

📐 Formulas You Might Be Asked to Write on the Board

Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)

RRF(d) = Σ [ 1 / (k + rank_i(d)) ]

Where k = 60 (standard smoothing constant), and rank_i(d) is the item's rank position in retrieval lane i (BGE-M3 or BM25).

Cosine Similarity (pgvector `<=>`)

cos(u, v) = (u · v) / ( ||u|| * ||v|| )

In pgvector, cosine distance is 1 - cos(u,v), queried using the <=> operator to find nearest neighbors.

Temporal IoU (Intersection over Union)

IoU = |S_pred ∩ S_gt| / |S_pred ∪ S_gt|

Measures temporal boundary accuracy. Must exceed 0.5 for a video procedural prediction to be counted as successful.

Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR@20)

MRR = (1 / |Q|) * Σ (1 / rank_first_relevant)

Trace scores 0.7759 Joint MRR@20, meaning the ground truth evidence is almost always in the #1 or #2 retrieved position.
